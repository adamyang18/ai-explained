Multimodal AI:
AI grows eyes and ears

One model that handles text, images, sound and video all at once.

Panel 1 · What it is

It doesn't just read. It can see, hear and talk

A “mode” (or modality) is just a form of information. Multimodal = can handle more than one form.

Panel 2 · How a picture gets in

Pictures get chopped into “building blocks” too

What the model actually sees is one long line of picture blocks + word blocks. Then it does what it always does best: make sense of it and predict what comes next.

Think of it like

A jigsaw puzzle. The picture is broken into small pieces, each with its own number and features. The model looks at those pieces together with your question.

What's really going on

The bigger and sharper the picture, the more blocks it turns into, and the more context space and money it costs. Video is a stream of pictures plus sound, so it's even pricier. Usually the model only samples some of the frames.

Common myth

“It sees pictures the way people do.” It understands images in a very different way. Some things you'd spot in a second, it gets wrong. See Panel 5.

Panel 3 · What it can do

The most common real-world jobs

JobExample
Reading documentsSnap a photo of an invoice, receipt or customs form → it pulls out the amount, date and item names
Reading chartsScreenshot a report → “Sum up which channel dropped the most this quarter”
Reading screensScreenshot of an app → find out why it's showing an error; AI agents operate a computer by looking at the screen
Live voicePhone customer service, speaking practice, live translation
Quality checksProduct photo → mark scratches or missing parts
Making contentProduct shots, ad images, voiceovers (usually done with special generation models)

Snapping a photo of a math problem and asking for a hint is a multimodal app too.

Panel 4 · How voice changed

From a three-person relay to one smooth move

That's why today's voice assistants are faster and sound more human. They can even tell if you're unsure or in a hurry.

Panel 5 · Limits

It can see, but it doesn't always see right

Pictures can make AI “hallucinate” too. For things like amounts, counts and key frames, a person should double-check.

Why this matters to you

When AI looks at a pitching video, it usually samples only a few frames per second, so it can miss the exact moment of release. That's why claims about one split-second (like where your hand was at release) should be checked frame by frame in the original video.

What's really going on

When a model handles video, it usually grabs just a few frames each second. It never actually sees the motion in between. Its description might be “filled in” from the frames before and after.

Common myth

“Giving it a screenshot is the same as giving it the data.” If you can get the original table or text, skip the screenshot. The raw data is more accurate and uses fewer tokens.

The short version

  1. Multimodal = one model that handles text, images, sound and video
  2. Pictures and sound get chopped into blocks too, lined up with the words
  3. Most common uses: reading documents, charts and screens, plus live voice
  4. Voice went from a three-step relay to one smooth move: faster and more human
  5. Counting, tiny text, positions and split-second details trip it up, so a person should check key answers
