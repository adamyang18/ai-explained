What AI costs:
tokens, prices and speed

The three things anyone asks before deciding if AI is worth the money.

Panel 1 · How you get charged

You pay per building block, and reading and writing are priced separately

Writing costs more than reading, because output has to be produced one token at a time, while input can be processed all at once, in parallel.

Panel 2 · Do the math

The prices are made-up values for the demo. Real prices vary a lot between companies and models. The method matters more than the numbers: number of chats × tokens per chat × price.

Think of it like

A taxi meter: there’s a base fare (the system instructions and docs it reads every single time) plus a per-kilometre charge (how long the answer is).

Easy to forget

Docs that RAG stuffs in, chat history that gets re-sent every turn, a reasoning model’s thinking notes, and the input at every step of an agent’s loop. These are often much bigger than the question and answer the user actually sees.

Common myth

“The model bill is the whole cost.” There’s also the people: building the system, cleaning up data, testing and keeping it running. In company projects, that part is often bigger.

Panel 3 · Two numbers for speed

How long until it starts talking, and how fast it talks

The bigger the model, the more it thinks, and the more docs you stuff in, the worse both numbers get.

Panel 4 · Quality, speed, cost

You can usually only pick two

A good setup isn’t the strongest model. It’s the fastest, cheapest combo that still passes the test.

Panel 6 · Is it worth it?

Don’t just quote the AI bill. Compare it to people.

Once the cost is clear, the next step goes back to Evals: proving you saved money without the quality dropping.

Remember this

  1. You pay per token; input and output are priced separately, and output costs more
  2. Monthly cost = calls × tokens per call × price. Don’t forget docs, history and thinking notes
  3. Speed comes down to two numbers: how soon it starts and how fast it talks
  4. Quality, speed, cost: pick two. Once it passes the test, pick the cheapest
  5. To judge if it’s worth it, compare with the cost of people doing the work
