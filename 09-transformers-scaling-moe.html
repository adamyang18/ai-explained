Under the hood:
Transformers, scaling laws and MoE

The three words that show up most in AI launch events and tech news.

Panel 1 · Transformer

The skeleton of almost every big AI model

At its core is a trick called attention.

Panel 2 · Attention

Every word looks back and asks: who should I pay attention to?

When the model reaches “it”, it gives every earlier word a score to decide who “it” is. Every word does this across the whole sentence, and that's how the meaning clicks together.

Think of it like

A team meeting where, before anyone speaks, they glance around the room, figure out which earlier comments matter most to what they're about to say, and focus on those.

Why it won

Older models read one word at a time, in order. That couldn't run in parallel, so training was slow. A Transformer lets all the words “look at” each other at the same time. That makes full use of GPUs working in parallel, which is what made training on massive amounts of data possible.

Link to the context window

Every word has to look at every other word. So if the text gets twice as long, this part of the work goes up about four times. That's one reason context windows can't be endless, and why long text costs more.

Panel 3 · Scaling laws

Throw in more resources, get predictably stronger

Research from around 2020 found that bigger models, more data and more computing power make the error rate fall along a predictable curve.

Think of it like

A farmer's fertilizer curve: putting in more raises the harvest, and you can roughly predict by how much. That's why big companies dare to spend huge sums training bigger models.

What's really going on

OpenAI's 2020 research first described this pattern. DeepMind's Chinchilla study in 2022 added that parameters and data have to grow together. Making the model bigger without enough data is a waste.

The current debate

High-quality text is running out. People in the industry argue about whether just going “bigger” is paying off less and less. Reasoning models that “think longer before answering” are another path to keep getting stronger.

Panel 4 · MoE: Mixture of Experts

Hire a hundred experts, wake up only a few each time

MoE = Mixture of Experts. The total number of parameters is huge, but only a small slice is used each time, so it's smart and still saves computing power.

Think of it like

A big hospital with hundreds of specialist doctors. Each patient only needs to see two or three of them. The hospital knows a lot overall, but treating one patient doesn't cost much.

Reading launch numbers

DeepSeek-V3 announced “671 billion total parameters, 37 billion activated per token.” The first number is about how much it knows. The second is about how fast and how expensive each step is. Mixtral, from Mistral, is another well-known MoE model.

Common myth

“MoE is efficient, so it's cheap to run.” It only computes a slice each time, but every expert still has to fit in the graphics card's memory. So the hardware needed to run it is still serious.

You don't need these words to use AI, but they help you follow what engineers and tech news are talking about.

The short version

  1. Transformer: the skeleton of big AI models, the T in GPT
  2. Attention: every word looks back over the sentence and decides what to focus on
  3. Scaling laws: more parameters, data and compute → predictably stronger
  4. MoE: lots of experts, only a few woken up each time; huge total parameters, small active parameters
