Open vs closed:
can you take the model home?

One of the first questions an AI company gets when it sells in other countries.

Panel 1 · Three levels of open

From “order off the menu” to “here’s the recipe and where we buy the ingredients”

Weights are the exact settings of those billions of knobs from lesson one. If you have the weights, you can run the model on your own machine.

Examples

Closed API: OpenAI’s GPT series, Anthropic’s Claude, Google’s flagship Gemini. Open weights: Meta’s Llama, Alibaba’s Qwen, DeepSeek and Mistral have all released models this way.

What’s really going on

The same company may do both: its flagship is API-only, while smaller or older versions get open weights. Whether something is “open” depends on the specific model, not the company.

Common myth

“Open weights means you can use it for business however you want.” Every model has its own license. Some limit how many users you can have, some limit what you can use it for. Read the actual license before using it commercially.

Panel 2 · Why open weights are useful

Your data stays home, and you can change whatever you want

The big selling point is control: where the data lives, how the model gets changed, and when to upgrade are all your call. Even if the maker stops offering the model, your copy keeps working.

Panel 3 · The catch

Takeout means you heat it up and do the dishes yourself

At low volume, calling an API is usually cheaper and easier. Running it yourself pays off when volume is high and the data is sensitive.

Panel 4 · How to choose

Pick based on the situation, not on team loyalty

SituationMore likely pick
Wants a quick trial, no AI engineering teamClosed API
Needs the strongest ability for complex reasoning or agentsClosed flagship (usually still a step ahead)
Data absolutely can’t leave the company / the countryOpen weights, run on your own servers
Huge volume of one simple task (like sorting or pulling out info)Small open-weights model + fine-tuning
Wants the best of bothMix: sensitive tasks run locally, hard problems go to an API

“A step ahead” changes fast. Check the latest test results before deciding, not your gut feeling.

Panel 5 · Selling abroad

When AI companies sell in other countries: three questions customers ask

Open weights give a ready-made answer: the model sits on your own servers, and your data never passes through us. That’s one reason many model makers selling abroad, including a lot of Chinese ones, go the open-weights route.

Think of it like

When you sell software, “install it on your own servers” (on-premises) gets through a customer’s security review more easily than “use our cloud.”

What’s really going on

Even when it runs on their own servers, the customer’s security team may still check where the model came from, what it was trained on, and what built-in tendencies it has. You need real answers ready, not just the word “open.”

Heads up

Rules in each country and companies’ buying policies change fast. For any specific customer, check the rules at the time. This page only covers the general idea.

Remember this

  1. Closed API = eat in; open weights = takeout; fully open = they hand you the recipe too
  2. “Open-source AI” usually means open weights, and business use depends on the license
  3. Open weights buy you control; the price is running it yourself
  4. Choose based on how sensitive the data is, how much you’ll use it, and what your team can handle
  5. When selling abroad, open weights are a strong answer to “where does my data go?”
